## Initial PLY findings

The file contains 500,000 Gaussians and 124,001,531 bytes. Its binary payload matches the declared vertex count and property layout. All stored float properties are finite. The layout includes DC color and 45 additional spherical harmonic coefficients, opacity, three scale parameters, and four rotation parameters.

Gaussian centers include extreme coordinate outliers (approximately -11,164 to +5,282 across axes). These are reconstruction coordinates, not established metres. The effect on rendering depends on opacity, size, and viewpoint; these bounds alone do not establish a poor reconstruction. Do not use the full bounding box to define the room or a collision map.

Stored rotation vectors are not unit length. Confirm exporter conventions and normalize quaternions when required by the renderer. Scale and opacity appear compatible with conventional log-scale/logit storage, but decoding must be confirmed against the exporter.

These are integrity checks, not evidence of visual or geometric fidelity. No Gaussian render or metric geometry comparison has yet been completed.

Reproduction is implemented in `Workflow/Evaluation/depth_check.py`, with instructions in `Workflow/Evaluation/README.md`. Three CPU tests cover opacity normalization/range-to-Z conversion, exclusion of evaluation views and bad timestamps from scale fitting, and error/bias summaries. A one-view GPU check and a full 25-view evaluation completed. A second full pass added RGB renders through the same cameras as a camera-model sanity check; depth statistics were unchanged.

### Method

- Use original FULL_OPENCV COLMAP cameras to render the checkpoint at native 1280×720. This preserves the distorted grid shared by recorded RGB and aligned depth; no depth interpolation or resizing is applied.
- Match depth by source timestamp using the shared selection, retaining at most 40 ms sensor-header residual. Of 37 appearance-evaluation views, 25 have eligible depth. Four have zero timestamp residual and the rest are approximately ±33.35 ms apart.
- Fit scene scale using the median of per-frame sparse depth-to-camera-Z ratios from **158 training views only**. The estimate is 0.754573 metres per reconstruction unit. Hold this fixed for all evaluation views. Do not confuse this with independent physical calibration.
- Convert the native accumulated weighted ray-hit distance to expected range by dividing by accumulated opacity, then convert to camera Z using the normalized camera ray's Z component and apply metric scale. The native source at the recorded revision was inspected to establish these conventions.
- Evaluate finite positive sensor depths up to 8 m, with finite positive predictions and rendered opacity at least 0.95. Keep all errors on this mask, including large outliers. Report overlap coverage to avoid hiding missing geometry.

The new dense Gaussian-depth results supersede any interpretation of the earlier 0.124 m sparse-point median as Gaussian surface accuracy. Do not permanently rescale the scene or define collision surfaces from these results alone.
